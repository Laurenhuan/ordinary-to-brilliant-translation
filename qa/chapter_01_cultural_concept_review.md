Scope: translation-decision preparation only. No item in this file is approved or locked. No Chapter 1 prose has been translated, and the frozen Chinese source remains unchanged.

## Proposed Chapter 1 cultural-concept policy

1. A Korean historical institution may retain a Korean term when a modern generic English label would mislead. Explain it briefly at first use and use the retained term thereafter, subject to human approval.
2. Confucian philosophical terms should preserve their conceptual tradition and relationship to one another. Do not replace them with easy modern near-synonyms that erase the philosophical context.
3. A proverb or quotation may use either a source-faithful project rendering or a conventional English proverb. The choice must be recorded because the latter may improve familiarity while reducing source-specific wording.
4. A provisional English candidate is not a verified quotation or a locked glossary entry. Every item below remains `needs_human_decision`.

## CULT-CH01-001 — 私塾

- **Chinese term:** 私塾
- **Context:** CH01-P006, P007, P013, P015, P016, and P024; the source describes the traditional education received by Lee Byung-chull and Chung Ju-yung, including instruction in classical Chinese texts.
- **Candidate translation:** `traditional Korean village school (seodang)` on first use; `seodang` thereafter
- **Alternative translation:** village school; traditional private academy; private school
- **Cultural explanation:** This is a historical Korean educational institution, not a modern fee-charging private school. Retaining `seodang` preserves the institution's cultural setting; the first-use explanation supplies reader orientation.
- **Recommendation:** Adopt the retained Korean term with a first-use explanation. Avoid `private school` because it creates a modern institutional association. Coordinate the decision with `文山亭书院 / Munsanjeong Seodang`.
- **Human decision:** Pending
- **Status:** `needs_human_decision`

## CULT-CH01-002 — 鸿儒

- **Chinese term:** 鸿儒
- **Context:** CH01-P006; the source describes Lee Hong-seok as an exceptionally learned figure in a Confucian intellectual setting, not as the holder of an official post.
- **Candidate translation:** `eminent Confucian scholar`
- **Alternative translation:** distinguished Confucian scholar; highly learned Confucian scholar; eminent scholar
- **Cultural explanation:** `鸿儒` carries both intellectual distinction and elevated praise. A plain `scholar` understates that force, while omitting `Confucian` would weaken the surrounding educational and classical context.
- **Recommendation:** Use `eminent Confucian scholar` for this occurrence. Keep the rule context-sensitive rather than treating the term as a formal title or automatic global replacement.
- **Human decision:** Pending
- **Status:** `needs_human_decision`

## CULT-CH01-003 — 地主

- **Chinese term:** 地主
- **Context:** CH01-P005 and P006; Chapter 1 describes landholding families and harvest capacity. It does not explicitly describe a modern landlord-tenant relationship.
- **Candidate translation:** `landowner` for an individual; `landowning family` for `地主家庭` or `地主之家`
- **Alternative translation:** landlord; large landowner; landed family
- **Cultural explanation:** In modern English, `landlord` commonly means a person who rents property to tenants. That implication is not automatically present in the source's description of rural wealth and family background.
- **Recommendation:** Apply the grammatical, context-sensitive pair `landowner / landowning family`. Use `landlord` only if a specific passage establishes a tenancy relationship; use `large landowner` only when the source explicitly supports scale.
- **Human decision:** Pending
- **Status:** `needs_human_decision`

## CULT-CH01-004 — 仁、和、乐

- **Chinese term:** 仁、和、乐
- **Context:** CH01-P025 and P026; the source presents the three terms together as the spiritual or philosophical realm described by the *Analects*.
- **Candidate translation:** `humaneness (ren), harmony (he), and joy (le)`
- **Alternative translation:** benevolence, harmony, and joy; humanity, harmony, and ritual joy; ren, he, and le with a collective gloss
- **Cultural explanation:** These are presented as Confucian concepts, not simply as everyday kindness, peace, and happiness. The English glosses should remain connected to the Chinese terms and preserve the three-part conceptual relationship.
- **Recommendation:** Use `humaneness (ren), harmony (he), and joy (le)` as the provisional first-use form. Human review must decide whether all three transliterations are necessary, how `乐` is understood in this passage, and whether later mentions use English glosses or retained terms.
- **Human decision:** Pending
- **Status:** `needs_human_decision`
