## Translator / Editor Notes

The following IDs are reserved in textual order. Their wording is authoritative and must not be paraphrased during translation.

### TN-CH03-001 — CH03-P013

The source names the institution here as “Gyeongseong Normal University.” Historical records show that Gyeongseong Normal School and Gyeongseong Women's Normal School operated during the colonial period, while Gyeongseong College of Education was formed after liberation in 1945. The exact institution intended by the source is unclear.

### TN-CH03-002 — CH03-P014

The source uses the name “Seoul Girls' Commercial School.” The school's historical record states that it was founded as Gyeongseong Girls' Commercial School in 1926 and was renamed Seoul Girls' Commercial High School in 1951.

### TN-CH03-003 — CH03-P030

The amounts are retained as stated in the source. Korea under Japanese rule used the Korean yen, issued by the Bank of Chosen; no modern currency conversion is attempted.

### TN-CH03-004 — CH03-P051

The source characterizes the 1980 restructuring as essentially an exchange between Daewoo Motor and Hyundai Heavy Industries. Korean National Archives records describe a broader series of heavy and chemical industry investment-adjustment measures covering automobiles, power-generation equipment, heavy electrical equipment, diesel engines, electronic switching equipment, copper smelting, and other sectors.

### TN-CH03-005 — CH03-P068

The source calls the 16 May 1961 seizure of power a “Military Revolution.” It is more commonly described in English-language historical references as the May 16 coup.

### TN-CH03-006 — CH03-P068

The source's “Korea Fertilizer incident” refers to the 1966 saccharin-smuggling scandal involving Korea Fertilizer Industries. Historical records show that Lee Byung-chull subsequently announced the transfer of his controlling shareholding and management rights to the government; the process was not a simple one-step handover of a factory.
