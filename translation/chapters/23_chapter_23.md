### CH23-P012

Source page: FB-P199

Translation:

Lee absolutely refused to play with anyone who ignored golf etiquette. A person who cheated on the score, lied, was late, made a loud commotion, or fell asleep snoring on the bathhouse floor after a round would never be invited to play with him again. Lee himself observed golf etiquette rigorously. When he arranged to play with others, he arrived before everyone else and waited for them; once on the course, he began without taking practice shots. He believed that making others wait imposed on them. His attitude was that “golf begins with etiquette and ends with etiquette.” Members of the Japanese business community greatly admired his conduct on the course.

### CH23-P021

Source page: FB-P201

Translation:

Lee did more than play golf well. He was also an expert on clubs, shoes, balls, courses, and other aspects of the game, and he always used the finest equipment. For wooden clubs, for example, he selected top-grade timber from the mountainous regions of northern Canada, specifically strips cut from the sun-facing side, said to yield the highest-quality wood. One of his favored club brands was Kennedy Smith. Each of its handmade clubs bore an engraved number; Lee used No. 57. At one point he owned more than five hundred clubs, all prestigious brands. He cared for these valuable clubs like works of art and polished them whenever he had time.

### CH23-P022

Source page: FB-P201

Translation:

Lee could be said to possess an aristocratic temperament. Yet personal taste alone did not account for his purchase of so many prestigious clubs. Whenever he encountered a first-rate product, he analyzed why it had achieved that status, examining what had been made well and how the product had become first-rate. He collected not only golf clubs but also pipes. Once he had reached his conclusion, his attachment to the object diminished. If someone praised one of his clubs, Lee promptly gave it to that person as a gift. He did the same with other possessions. Chairman Lee Kun-hee later said that if his father had kept all the things he gave away, the collection would have been enough to open a museum. Lee Byung-chull merely smiled.
